---

## 1. Purpose test — why we want to process the data

CoachCarter Ltd records dashcam footage in tuition vehicles for four legitimate purposes:

1. **Safety.** Recording incidents involving learners, instructors, or third parties to support investigation and prevention. This includes both road-traffic incidents (collisions, near-misses, third-party negligence) and in-cabin safety concerns (inappropriate behaviour by either party, allegations of misconduct, disputed events).

2. **Quality assurance.** Reviewing lesson conduct in cases where a learner raises a complaint about an instructor, or vice versa. Without an objective record, such disputes resolve on the basis of contested verbal accounts, which is unsatisfactory for both parties and for CCL as the franchisor.

3. **Instructor coaching and development.** Selected footage may be reviewed with the instructor concerned to support their professional development — e.g. handling of difficult learner moments, lesson structure, or technical instruction. Used only with the instructor's awareness.

4. **Insurance evidence.** Supporting any insurance claim arising from a tuition incident — providing the insurer with first-hand evidence of events.

These purposes are lawful, specific, and align with established industry practice for driving-instruction businesses. The Information Commissioner's Office has explicitly recognised dashcam use in driving instruction as a well-supported application of legitimate interests when used for safety, quality, and training.

**Content creation for social media is NOT a purpose covered by this assessment.** Any use of dashcam footage for marketing, social-media content, or external publication is processed under a separate lawful basis (explicit consent — Article 6(1)(a)) via a dedicated learner-consent flow. The legitimate-interests basis described in this document covers only the four internal purposes listed above.

---

## 2. Necessity test — is processing necessary for the purpose?

Dashcam recording is necessary because no less-intrusive alternative achieves the stated purposes adequately. Alternatives considered and rejected:

- **Instructor verbal recall.** Unreliable for disputed events; provides no objective record. Fails purposes 1, 2, and 4 entirely.
- **Audio-only recording.** Would not capture critical visual context — steering inputs, mirror checks, road conditions, third-party vehicle behaviour, pedestrian behaviour, or dual-control intervention timing. Materially weaker for purposes 1, 3, and 4.
- **External-facing camera only (no cabin view).** Would miss in-cabin learner behaviour, instructor conduct, and the timing of instructor intervention. Critically, would not address safety concern (1) where the alleged incident is in-cabin (e.g. inappropriate behaviour from either party). For these scenarios, in-cabin recording is the *primary* protective measure for both learner and instructor.
- **Manual incident reporting.** Relies on memory and self-reporting; unreliable for disputed events; offers no protection where one party denies the event occurred.

The processing captures the minimum information required: vehicle interior + forward-facing road view, during lesson hours only. Recording stops when the lesson ends. No data beyond this is collected for these purposes.

The interior-facing element of the recording is specifically necessary because it is the only mechanism that protects both the learner *and* the instructor from contested allegations of inappropriate behaviour. Removing it would leave both parties unprotected in the scenarios most likely to produce serious disputes.

## 3. Balancing test — does our interest outweigh the individual's rights and freedoms?

CCL's interests are weighed against the learner's reasonable expectation of privacy in a vehicle they are not the owner of, during a paid commercial service.

### Mitigations applied

- **Disclosure before recording starts.** Learners are informed via (a) a visible disclosure sticker inside the vehicle, (b) a dedicated section in the published privacy policy at coachcarter.uk/privacy, and (c) verbal mention at the first-lesson briefing. No recording occurs without prior disclosure to the learner.

- **Retention limited to what is necessary.** Standard retention is 30 days from capture, after which footage is deleted. Incident-flagged footage may be retained for up to 12 months to support insurance claims. Footage tied to active legal or insurance proceedings is retained for the duration of those proceedings only. Most footage is deleted within days of capture as part of the data controller's routine clearance discipline.

- **Storage location.** Footage is stored on the data controller's (Fraser Carter's) secured personal computer. It is not uploaded to CCL platform infrastructure, not uploaded to cloud storage, and not transferred to any third-party service. This minimises the attack surface and data-flow complexity, and limits the population of people who could ever access the footage.

- **Access restriction.** Footage is accessible only to the data controller (Fraser Carter) and the instructor who delivered the lesson. Instructor access is permitted specifically for instructor coaching and development purposes (purpose 3) and for review of lessons they personally taught. No wider access is granted without a documented and justified reason.

- **No public publication without separate explicit consent.** Any external use of footage — including social-media content, marketing material, or publication of any kind — requires the explicit prior consent of every learner appearing in the footage, granted via a dedicated consent flow with full informed details. This is a separate lawful basis (consent under Article 6(1)(a)) and is documented separately. Refusing this content-publication consent has no impact on lesson availability, lesson pricing, or any aspect of the tuition service (other than forfeiting the discrete 5% discount tied to that consent).

- **Subject access requests.** Learners may request a copy of dashcam footage in which they appear via a Subject Access Request. CCL fulfils such requests within 30 days, in line with statutory obligations.

- **Right to object.** Under Article 21 UK GDPR, learners have the right to object to processing under legitimate interests. Where a learner objects to dashcam recording, CCL will assess the objection on its facts. Because dashcam recording is a fleet-wide non-negotiable safety policy applied uniformly to all tuition vehicles, CCL is unable to provide tuition that is not subject to recording. A learner who objects and refuses to proceed under this policy will, regrettably, be unable to take lessons with CCL. This position is taken because (i) the recording is a primary safeguarding measure protecting both parties, and (ii) selectively disabling it for individual learners would compromise its protective function.

- **Children.** Learners under 18 are subject to additional caution: footage retained for the minimum necessary period; never used for content-publication purposes (which are 18-and-over only); processed with proportionate restraint.

### Outcome of the balancing test

Considering the four purposes (safety, quality, coaching, insurance), the necessity of the processing, and the mitigations applied, CCL's legitimate interest in recording dashcam footage is judged to outweigh the privacy intrusion to the learner. The intrusion is proportionate: limited in time (lesson duration only), limited in location (inside the tuition vehicle), limited in retention (30 days standard), limited in access (controller + instructor only), and accompanied by full transparency and clear opt-out routes (cancel tuition).

The interior-facing element specifically — which is the more intrusive component of the recording — is justified by the protective function it serves *for the learner* as well as for the instructor. A learner alleging inappropriate behaviour by an instructor is materially better protected by interior-facing dashcam evidence than by their own verbal account; the same is true in reverse. Removing this element to reduce intrusion would weaken the protection it offers to the very people whose privacy is at stake.

---

## Decision

CCL is satisfied that legitimate interests is an appropriate lawful basis for the processing described in this document. The assessment is documented, retained, and will be reviewed annually or sooner if material circumstances change (e.g. fleet expansion, new processing purposes, change of storage location, change of retention policy, regulatory guidance update).
