# CoachCarter 30-Day Email Sequence Reference

> Status: Living strategy and accountability document
>
> Last updated: 18 August 2026
>
> Use this document when creating lead magnets, videos, email copy, nurture sequences, offers, or the supporting email automation. Future work should update this document when a strategic decision changes.

## 1. Purpose

The 30-day sequence is intended to turn an interested learner or parent from a curious prospect into someone who understands and trusts the CoachCarter approach enough to decide whether coaching is right for them.

It is not simply a newsletter or a month of disconnected driving tips. It should deliberately:

1. Deliver the resource the person requested.
2. Create an unexpectedly useful early experience.
3. Learn what the person most wants help with.
4. Shift the beliefs that affect how they choose a driving coach.
5. Demonstrate the mechanisms CoachCarter uses to produce progress.
6. Support those claims with credible proof.
7. Invite the right people to take a clear next step.

The prospect's underlying evaluation during the sequence is:

> Is this relationship useful to me?

Every email and asset should help them answer that question.

The webinar supplied two headline figures:

- 43% of buyers are interested but are not “now” buyers.
- An average of 47 minutes of intentional content consumption can move someone towards a purchase.

These numbers are working hypotheses from the webinar. Their original research source has not yet been identified or independently validated. Do not publish them as CoachCarter claims without locating and checking the underlying evidence.

For internal planning, the useful principle is:

> Many good prospects need time and meaningful exposure before they are ready to act. Build enough valuable, intentional content to let trust and understanding develop.

The aim is not to make every person consume exactly 47 minutes. Quality, relevance, completion, replies, and resulting actions matter more than manufacturing watch time.

## 3. CoachCarter's desired result and three pillars

### Desired result

Help someone learn to drive safely and independently.

Passing the practical test is an important milestone, but the wider outcome is a capable driver who can make good decisions without an instructor beside them.

### Three development pillars

| Code | Pillar | Working meaning |
|---|---|---|
| A | Knowledge | Understanding the road, the rules, the situation, and why a decision is appropriate. |
| B | Safety | Seeing, anticipating, and managing risk while making calm decisions. |
| C | Skill | Controlling the vehicle and executing driving tasks accurately, smoothly, and consistently. |

Keep the A/B/C mapping consistent in forms, emails, replies, tags, and automation:

- A = Knowledge
- B = Safety
- C = Skill

These pillars give the content library and the sequence a stable structure. A lead magnet, belief, mechanism, proof item, video, or email should normally have one primary pillar even when it touches the other two.

## 4. The content engine: beliefs, mechanisms, and proof

All core content should be built from three ingredients.

### 4.1 Manifesto: teach people what to want

The manifesto is CoachCarter's collection of useful contrarian beliefs. Each belief follows this shape:

> Most of the industry says, or most learners assume, **X**. Based on my experience, I believe **Y**, because **Z**.

The purpose is not to be contrary for attention. A belief belongs in the manifesto only when:

- it genuinely reflects how CoachCarter coaches;
- it helps a learner make a better decision or achieve a better result;
- Fraser can explain why it is true;
- a mechanism or behaviour supports it; and
- proof can eventually be attached to it.

#### Belief development table

| Pillar | Common industry/client belief | CoachCarter belief | Why it matters | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Knowledge | To be discovered | To be developed | To be developed | Open |
| Safety | To be discovered | To be developed | To be developed | Open |
| Skill | To be discovered | To be developed | To be developed | Open |

Candidate prompts for future belief discovery—not approved manifesto statements:

- What do learners focus on because it feels measurable, even though it does not produce independence?
- What advice is commonly repeated by instructors but fails in real traffic?
- What do people misunderstand about confidence, mistakes, manoeuvres, roundabouts, mock tests, or test readiness?
- Where does memorising a routine become weaker than understanding the situation?
- What does a learner think they need more of when they actually need a different kind of practice or feedback?
- What would Fraser refuse to promise or teach even if competitors routinely do so?

### 4.2 Mechanisms: show how the result is produced

A mechanism is a named or clearly explained CoachCarter process that helps a learner improve Knowledge, Safety, or Skill.

Examples could include assessment methods, feedback loops, lesson structures, reflection exercises, progress tracking, mock-test processes, decision-making frameworks, or ways of practising a specific driving situation. Do not invent a branded name merely to make an ordinary action sound proprietary. The mechanism must describe something real and useful.

#### Mechanism inventory

| Mechanism | Primary pillar | Problem it solves | How it works | Supporting belief | Current status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| To be discovered | Knowledge / Safety / Skill |  |  |  | Existing / planned |

For each mechanism, answer:

1. What learner problem does this solve?
2. What happens before, during, and after using it?
3. Why is it more effective than the default approach?
4. What observable change tells us it worked?
5. Can Fraser demonstrate it in a video or story?

### 4.3 Proof: make the beliefs credible

Proof can come from three sources:

1. Client experience: learner stories, feedback, progress, decisions, or results.
2. Fraser's own experience: teaching observations, mistakes, lessons learned, or repeatable patterns.
3. Third-party evidence: research, official guidance, expert examples, or relevant stories.

#### Proof bank

| Proof item | Source type | Pillar | Belief/mechanism supported | Permission/source checked? | Usable formats |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| To be collected | Client / Fraser / third party | Knowledge / Safety / Skill |  | No | Email / video / page |

Never invent testimonials, outcomes, learner details, scarcity, statistics, or research. An anonymised learner story still needs to be accurate and handled in line with the learner's permissions and CoachCarter's privacy commitments.

## 5. The CoachCarter belief map

The workbook's belief-map example places the desired result at the centre, supporting characteristics around it, and mechanisms/proof around the outside. The CoachCarter adaptation is:

```text
                         LEARN TO DRIVE
                  SAFELY AND INDEPENDENTLY

             Knowledge      Safety       Skill
                  \            |           /
                   \     Beliefs to hold   /
                    \          |          /
                     Real mechanisms used
                              |
                 Client / Fraser / third-party proof
```

This map should eventually answer:

- What does an ideal learner need to believe about Knowledge?
- What does an ideal learner need to believe about Safety?
- What does an ideal learner need to believe about Skill?
- Which CoachCarter mechanism makes each belief tangible?
- Which proof item makes each claim credible?

The belief map is the source for content. The email calendar should not be filled first and justified afterwards.

## 6. Asset inventory and the “banger” standard

Every substantial asset should be recorded before it enters the sequence.

| Asset | Pillar | Belief shift | Mechanism | Proof | Format | Length | Banger? | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|
| To be created |  | From ___ to ___ |  |  | Video / guide / tool |  | No | Idea |

An asset is sequence-ready only when it passes these questions:

- Is the opening immediately relevant to the person who opted in?
- Does it produce a useful insight or practical win?
- Is the advice specific enough to apply?
- Does it shift one important belief rather than cover a topic vaguely?
- Does it demonstrate a real CoachCarter mechanism or credible proof?
- Is the production quality good enough that it increases trust?
- Would Fraser be happy if this were the only CoachCarter content someone saw?

If the honest answer to the final question is no, it is not a “banger” yet.

### Video progression

Sequence videos should generally:

1. Start short enough to earn attention while still delivering genuine value.
2. Become longer only after the earlier assets have earned trust.
3. Start with attractive, immediate, high-leverage wins.
4. Progress towards more advanced, nuanced, or granular teaching.
5. Make the intended belief shift explicit during planning.

A useful planning sentence is:

> Before this video, the prospect may believe **X**. After watching, they should understand or believe **Y**, because they have seen **mechanism/proof Z**.

## 7. Sequence architecture

The sequence uses “give” content and three deliberate types of ask:

1. Early sorting: learn what the subscriber wants help with.
2. Mid-run SPEAR: make a short, personal invitation and expect a reply.
3. Closing campaign: invite qualified prospects to the current offer.

### Working cadence

The exact schedule remains testable. The initial CoachCarter shape should be approximately 10 emails across 30 days, with an optional final reminder if it is genuinely useful.

| Day | Purpose | Main action | Asset/CTA |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0 | Deliver, delight, and sort | Deliver the requested lead magnet, add one fast win, and ask A/B/C | Lead magnet + short supporting asset + reply/category links |
| 1 | Morning-after message | Check that the resource arrived and ask one easy question | Reply |
| 4 | Value/belief shift 1 | Continue with the entry pillar chosen by the lead magnet or reply | Short high-impact asset |
| 8 | Value/belief shift 2 | Deepen or complete the first topic cluster | Asset using mechanism/proof |
| 13 | SPEAR | Make a short, neutral invitation to work together | Reply yes/no |
| 17 | Value/belief shift 3 | Move into a second pillar or key objection | Medium-length asset |
| 22 | Value/belief shift 4 | Build understanding across the wider CoachCarter method | Deeper asset |
| 28 | Invitation | Announce the genuine offer, audience, outcome, capacity, and close | Reply/apply/book |
| 29 | Details | Explain fit, plan, proof, terms, and next step | Reply/apply/book |
| 30 | Close | Restate the outcome, qualification, first steps, and real deadline | Reply/apply/book |

Optional: a short Day 30 final-hours reply email may be tested later. Do not add it automatically merely because the workbook contains one.

The workbook includes both a Day 13 SPEAR template and a sample path that places the SPEAR on Day 15. Fraser's working note is Day 13, so Day 13 is the current default. Treat timing as a test variable, not doctrine.

### Topic clusters

Middle content can use several touches around a coherent topic such as roundabouts or manoeuvres. A cluster should point to one strong anchor asset or a tightly connected set of assets rather than scattering attention across unrelated tips.

A cluster can:

- introduce the common problem;
- teach the underlying belief or decision principle;
- demonstrate the mechanism;
- provide proof or an example; and
- invite the person to apply it or reply.

Not every day needs an email. “Three or four days on roundabouts” can mean a topic remains the current focus across that period, not necessarily three or four sends.

## 8. Days 0–1: deliver, delight, and sort

### Day 0 structure

1. Deliver: provide exactly what the subscriber requested immediately.
2. Delight: add a useful idea and a supporting video/tool that helps them get the desired result faster.
3. Sort: ask which of the three areas would be most useful next.

#### CoachCarter working template

**Subject:** [Name of the resource they requested]

Hi [First name],

Here is [lead magnet] you asked for: [link].

One of the quickest ways to improve [relevant result] is [useful move or belief]. I made this short [video/tool] to walk you through it: [link].

What would you most like help with right now?

- A — Knowledge
- B — Safety
- C — Skill

Hit reply with A, B, or C and I will send you something useful.

[Fraser's sign-off]

The additional asset must complement the promised lead magnet, not delay or obscure it.

### Day 1 structure

The second email should feel human and be easy to answer. It can ask whether the resource arrived, then ask one useful sorting question.

Possible questions include:

- Did the [resource] come through okay?
- Are you learning at the moment, choosing an instructor, or getting ready to restart?
- Which feels hardest right now: knowing what to do, spotting risk early, or controlling the car consistently?
- Is your practical test already booked, or are you building towards it without a date yet?

Ask one question, not a survey.

## 9. Lead-magnet routing

The lead magnet reveals the person's immediate interest. Honour that context before moving them into the general sequence.

Example:

```text
Roundabout lead magnet
    -> immediate roundabout resource
    -> first supporting email/video remains on roundabouts
    -> tag primary entry as Safety, Skill, or Knowledge as appropriate
    -> use reply/click behaviour to refine the tag
    -> rejoin the core sequence after the tailored opening
```

Rules:

- Start in the area the person explicitly asked about.
- Provide meaningful value in that area early.
- Do not force everyone through an identical opening regardless of their request.
- Rejoin the core sequence once the promise of the lead magnet has been properly fulfilled.
- Carry the lead magnet's terminology into later emails and the offer so the journey feels continuous.
- Preserve the original source and lead-magnet tag even if their primary interest later changes.

## 10. The mid-run SPEAR

SPEAR means:

> Short. Personal. Expects A Reply.

The SPEAR is deliberately plain. It is not a disguised long sales letter.

### Working pattern

**Subject:** Working together

Hi [First name],

Would you like some help to [neutral, relevant benefit]?

Fraser

Examples of neutral benefits might include becoming safer and more confident at roundabouts, getting ready for a practical test, or building consistent control. The wording must match what CoachCarter can genuinely help with and what is known about the subscriber.

The SPEAR's job is to start a conversation. A lack of reply is not permission to pretend the person expressed interest.

## 11. Days 28–30: the invitation campaign

The closing campaign should use the same beliefs, mechanisms, and language introduced earlier. It should feel like the natural next step, not a sudden change of personality.

Only use real capacity, dates, deadlines, prices, outcomes, and risk-reversal terms. Never manufacture scarcity.

### Day 28: invitation

**Subject:** [Month] [offer/program]

Hi [First name],

I am opening [real number] places in [offer/program] in [month] for [specific learners] who want [desired outcome].

Would you like to join us?

Fraser

### Day 29: details

Explain:

- how many genuine places remain and when applications genuinely close;
- the simple promise or objective;
- what the learner will do;
- the CoachCarter mechanisms involved;
- relevant proof without exaggeration;
- honest disqualifiers and qualifiers;
- the phases or first steps;
- the price and included service in plain language;
- any genuine risk reversal; and
- the single next action.

The offer should build on terminology already used in the lead magnet and sequence.

### Day 30: close

Restate:

- who the offer is for;
- the desired outcome;
- what will happen first;
- the real closing time; and
- how to respond.

Keep the final call to action simple. Depending on the offer, this might be replying, applying, booking an assessment, or choosing a lesson slot. Do not introduce several competing actions.

## 12. Content-quality and accountability rules

Before approving any sequence email, answer:

1. Which pillar is primary: Knowledge, Safety, or Skill?
2. Which prospect belief is this trying to change or strengthen?
3. Is the belief genuinely part of Fraser's coaching philosophy?
4. Which mechanism demonstrates that CoachCarter can act on the belief?
5. What proof supports it?
6. Why is this useful at this exact point in the sequence?
7. Does it match the lead magnet or known interest of this subscriber?
8. What is the one intended action: consume, practise, reply, apply, or book?
9. Is every claim, testimonial, deadline, and place count true?
10. Is the content strong enough to be someone's only impression of CoachCarter?

If an email cannot answer questions 1–6, it is probably filler.

### Sequence-level review

Before launch, check that:

- the requested resource is delivered immediately;
- the first value is relevant to the opt-in source;
- the three pillars are represented intentionally rather than mechanically;
- each substantial asset has a planned belief shift;
- asset lengths generally build as trust grows;
- the sequence contains credible proof from more than one source type;
- the SPEAR is genuinely short and reply-oriented;
- the offer uses familiar sequence language;
- all deadlines and scarcity are real;
- every marketing email has a functioning unsubscribe route;
- replies reach a monitored inbox; and
- a booking, conversion, unsubscribe, bounce, or complaint triggers the correct exit behaviour.

## 13. Measurement and learning loop

Do not treat opens as the main measure of success. Track:

- lead-magnet requests;
- successful deliveries and bounces;
- A/B/C sort replies or clicks;
- direct replies to each email;
- asset clicks;
- video starts, meaningful watch time, and completions where consented analytics permits;
- SPEAR replies;
- applications or bookings;
- conversion by lead-magnet source and primary pillar;
- unsubscribes and complaints; and
- time from opt-in to booking.

The 47-minute idea should be tested using actual aggregate engagement and conversion data. Useful questions include:

- Does greater intentional consumption correlate with more replies or bookings?
- Which assets contribute to conversion rather than merely attract clicks?
- At what point does engagement fall away?
- Which lead magnets attract good-fit learners rather than just high opt-in volume?
- Which pillar is most associated with a booking decision?

Correlation is not proof of causation. Use the data to improve the sequence, not to claim a universal threshold.

## 14. Future technical implementation requirements

CoachCarter can reuse its current Resend/SMTP delivery, Neon database, send logging, and Vercel cron patterns. The marketing sequence still needs a dedicated layer containing:

- durable marketing contacts with normalised email addresses;
- explicit consent timestamp, source, wording/version, and lead-magnet ID;
- A/B/C interest tags;
- sequence definitions and steps;
- per-contact enrollments with next-send time;
- an idempotent send ledger preventing duplicates;
- immediate one-click unsubscribe and durable suppression;
- bounce and complaint handling;
- rate-limited public opt-in endpoints;
- multi-tenant `school_id` scoping;
- retention, export, deletion/anonymisation, and audit handling; and
- exit rules for booking, conversion, unsubscribe, bounce, or complaint.

The existing `enquiries.marketing_consent` flag is useful historical input but is not, by itself, a complete mailing-list or suppression record.

## 15. How future AI sessions should use this document

When Fraser asks for a lead magnet, video, email, offer, or sequence change:

1. Read this document first.
2. Identify the primary pillar and the relevant belief, mechanism, and proof.
3. Flag when an idea does not map to the strategy instead of silently forcing it in.
4. Never invent proof, statistics, learner stories, prices, availability, deadlines, or programme details.
5. Keep webinar claims marked as hypotheses until their sources are validated.
6. Preserve A = Knowledge, B = Safety, and C = Skill.
7. Start follow-up in the area promised by the lead magnet.
8. Prefer one strong asset and one clear action over several weak links or CTAs.
9. Use plain, human language that sounds like Fraser rather than generic marketing copy.
10. Update the belief map, mechanism inventory, proof bank, asset inventory, cadence, or decision log when a real decision is made.

If Fraser proposes content that is merely an isolated driving tip, ask or determine:

> Which belief does this shift, which CoachCarter mechanism does it reveal, and why would it help an ideal learner choose the right coach?
